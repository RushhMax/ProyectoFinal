\chapter*{Abstract}
\hrule \bigskip \vspace*{1cm}

Deep learning models for financial time series prediction achieve high accuracy but do not reveal which information in the historical window determined each prediction. Existing explainability methods, such as SHAP and LIME, were formulated assuming independent variables and produce inconsistent explanations when applied to time series, because they ignore the temporal dependence between observations. This work proposes a model-agnostic temporal explainability framework that, given an input window $x \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times V}$, produces an importance matrix $\hat{\alpha} \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times V}$ indicating how much each variable contributed at each point in the historical window to the prediction being explained. The framework operates through temporally ordered perturbations and communicates with any model exclusively through its prediction function, without accessing weights, gradients, or internal architecture.

The framework is evaluated on three models of different computational nature (LSTM, Transformer, and Random Forest), trained on historical data from the S\&P~500 index. Results show that the framework correctly identifies relevant inputs when ground truth is known (AUP\,$=0.9960$), runs each explanation in 17.6\,ms, between $22{\times}$ and $30{\times}$ faster than Shapley Value Sampling under identical experimental conditions, and produces statistically non-trivial attributions ($p<10^{-300}$) with appropriate differentiation between model types (Spearman $\rho=0.748$ between neural models versus $\rho<0.40$ against the ensemble model), confirming that the framework captures model-specific behavior rather than artifacts of the explanation procedure. An honest investigation of a Random-Forest-specific consideration, anchored in its tree structure, is also reported, aimed at reducing its higher sensitivity to the reference vector, together with the actual result obtained even though it did not confirm the initial hypothesis.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} financial time series, post-hoc explainability, model-agnostic, temporal perturbations, feature importance, LSTM, Transformer, Random Forest.
